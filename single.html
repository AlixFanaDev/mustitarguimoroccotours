Desert diary · April 12, 2025

A first-timer’s guide
to the Sahara.

The magic of the Moroccan desert is not only in the dunes — it is in the silence, the sky, the hospitality, and the journey itself.

By Musti Targui5 min readMerzouga · Erg Chebbi
31°05′N, 4°01′W

A Sahara trip is often the part of a Morocco vacation people remember for the rest of their lives. It is the feeling of walking into an endless landscape, watching the sun disappear behind the dunes, and then seeing the dark sky fill with stars.

For first-time visitors, the desert can feel both vast and intimate at the same time. The days are warm and open; the nights are quiet and full of light. There is no rush, no noise, and no pressure to do anything except notice what is around you.

That is why so many travelers say the best part of Morocco is not a single monument, but the feeling of being carried by the land itself.

Sunset over the dunes

In Merzouga, many travelers begin with a camel trek across the dunes before sunset. The ride is gentle but memorable, and the view from the top of the dune is truly unforgettable. As golden hour fades into twilight, the colors become softer and more dramatic, and the desert seems to glow from within.

Evening usually brings a camp, a tagine dinner, music, and a chance to sit outside under a sky full of stars. This is where the desert becomes more than a landscape — it becomes an experience rooted in hospitality and calm.

Come prepared, not exhausted

For those planning their first visit, the best advice is simple: travel with time, not speed. Give yourself room to pause, to watch the light change, and to enjoy the slower rhythm of the desert. It is a place that asks you to be present.
